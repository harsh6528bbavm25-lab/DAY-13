## 📖 Project Description
This project demonstrates how **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)** can be used to provide answers based on an approved source document rather than relying only on general AI knowledge.

The activity uses official **Air India baggage information** to compare ungrounded and grounded AI responses and evaluate whether generated claims are actually supported by the source.

## 🎯 Objectives
- Understand the concept of Retrieval-Augmented Generation.
- Compare ungrounded and source-grounded AI answers.
- Verify AI claims against an approved source.
- Identify unsupported or invented information.
- Check claim–source matches and surrounding context.
- Handle missing information without guessing.
- Understand the importance of human verification in aviation.

## ✈️ Source Used
**Source:** Air India – Lost or Damaged Baggage

The source covers delayed, lost and damaged baggage, including baggage reporting, Property Irregularity Reports (PIR), essential expense reimbursement, delivery timelines, tracking and liability.

## 🔍 Ungrounded vs Grounded Answers

### Ungrounded Answer
The initial AI answer was generated without the specific source document. Although it contained plausible airline-policy information, its claims could not be verified against the Air India source.

### Grounded Answer
After providing the source, the AI answer was restricted to information supported by the document. Claims were checked against the source and missing information was explicitly identified rather than guessed.
